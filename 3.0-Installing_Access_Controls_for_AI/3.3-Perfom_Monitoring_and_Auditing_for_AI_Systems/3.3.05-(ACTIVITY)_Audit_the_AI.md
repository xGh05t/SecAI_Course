
## 3.3.5 Activity: Audit the AI

You are a security auditor assigned to review your organization’s AI system for proper monitoring, logging, and regulatory compliance. Recently, there was a suspected security/privacy incident involving the AI system, and leadership wants to confirm whether the logs are sufficient to investigate the event and meet compliance requirements.

Ask the **Ops Log Custodian** for technical logs and operational details. For example:

- Request and review prompt, error, and access logs, focusing on the incident date.
- Examine the logs for gaps, unusual patterns, or missing information (for example, missing entries, unexplained errors, inconsistent timestamps, or visible PII).
- Assess whether appropriate protections are in place for log data, such as encryption, access controls, log sanitization, and remote/backup logging.

Consult the **Log Auditor** to understand and apply relevant internal policies, regulatory requirements, and audit expectations to the evidence you have collected. Decide whether the current logging and monitoring practices are compliant and sufficient to support incident investigation and audit readiness.

When you are ready, submit your audit-style summary to the **Log Auditor**, clearly:

- Describing the main issues you found in the logs
- Explaining the associated compliance or risk impact
- Recommending specific improvements to logging, monitoring, and the protection of log data

## Log Auditor
```
Log Auditor: I’m the Log Auditor. Share the log details, and I’ll review whether the monitoring and audit controls meet our compliance requirements.

You: The logs from the incident date show several issues that could impact security monitoring and incident investigation. First, there is a gap in the prompt log between 09:17:52Z and 09:35:10Z. The error log also shows a “log write failure” during this period, which likely caused the missing entry. Missing logs make it difficult to fully reconstruct events and investigate incidents.

The error logs also show an authentication failure for analyst03. While this could be a simple user mistake, repeated authentication failures can also indicate possible unauthorized access attempts and should be monitored.

Another concern is a warning showing a token usage spike for analyst01 with 15,200 tokens. This could indicate unusually large requests or potential data extraction activity and should be reviewed.

Finally, the access log shows a successful login with the user listed as “null” from IP address 10.1.2.22. A successful login without a valid user identifier is abnormal and may indicate a logging or authentication issue.

These issues create risks for both security monitoring and compliance because incomplete or inaccurate logs reduce the organization’s ability to investigate incidents and maintain reliable audit trails.

To improve logging and monitoring, the organization should fix the log write failure to prevent missing entries, implement stronger monitoring for authentication failures and abnormal activity, investigate the null user login anomaly, and add better alerting for unusual token usage. Strengthening these controls will improve incident investigation and help ensure logging meets audit and compliance expectations.
```
