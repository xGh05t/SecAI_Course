
# 2.2.9 Activity: Defensive Policy Builder

Your company is deploying a new AI-powered HR chatbot that handles sensitive employee data (payroll, personal details, HR requests) and is central to employee services. The chatbot must operate securely and reliably to protect both employees and the organization.

In this activity, you will work with a Defense Policy Mentor to design a layered AI defensive policy for this HR chatbot. For each of these three layers—Model, Gateway, and Testing & Validation—you’ll be prompted to select two controls from the lists below and provide a 1–2 sentence, scenario-specific justification for each choice. If you need definitions or clarification on any control, you can consult ResourceBot.

**Model Controls**: Output Filtering & PII Masking, Role-Based Access Control (Authorization), Input Validation, Prompt Templates (Injection Prevention), Model/API Exposure Limiting

**Gateway Controls**: Prompt Firewall (Centralized Filtering), Rate Limiting, Token Limit Enforcement, File/Input Size Limit, Endpoint Access Controls

**Testing & Validation Controls**: Prompt Injection Testing, Bias Testing, Penetration/Red Team Testing, Data Poisoning / Supply Chain Testing, Guardrail Bypass Simulation

## Defense Policy Mentor

```
Defense Policy Mentor: Welcome! I’m your Defense Policy Mentor, a senior member of our security team. I’ll guide you through designing a robust defensive policy for our new AI-powered HR chatbot.

I’ll ask you, one layer at a time, to choose the most important security controls for the model, gateway, and testing & validation layers, and to briefly justify each choice based on the specific risks and business needs of an HR chatbot.

When you’re ready to begin, type start and press Enter.

You: start

Defense Policy Mentor: Let's begin with the AI model itself. Please name the single most important security control you would apply at the model layer for this HR chatbot and briefly justify why it's vital for our scenario.

You: Role-Based Access Control ensures the HR chatbot only returns information appropriate to the authenticated user's role (employee, manager, or HR administrator). This prevents unauthorized users from accessing sensitive employee records such as payroll or personal HR data.

Defense Policy Mentor: Thank you for your answer. Role-Based Access Control is certainly a foundational security measure, and your justification shows awareness of the sensitivity of HR data and the importance of least privilege.

Great. Now provide the next most important model security control and its scenario-specific justification.

You: Output filtering and PII masking prevent the chatbot from exposing sensitive employee data such as Social Security numbers, home addresses, or payroll details. This protects employee privacy and helps maintain compliance with data protection policies.

Defense Policy Mentor: Excellent. Output filtering and PII masking are crucial for a system handling personal information, particularly in an HR context. Your justification correctly addresses privacy and regulatory compliance.

Moving to the gateway, what is the most important security control you would implement here to protect the chatbot? Briefly justify your choice for our HR use case.

You: A prompt firewall filters and sanitizes user prompts before they reach the AI model, blocking prompt injection attempts or malicious instructions designed to bypass system safeguards. This protects the HR chatbot from manipulation attempts that could expose sensitive HR data.

Defense Policy Mentor: That's an effective choice. A prompt firewall is essential at the gateway level to help ensure the model isn't exploited through crafted prompts, which is particularly vital with HR data.

Please name one more critical gateway-level control and briefly explain why it will help protect this AI system.

You: Rate limiting restricts how frequently users can send requests to the chatbot. This helps prevent automated abuse, brute-force attempts, or large-scale probing designed to extract confidential employee information.

Defense Policy Mentor: Well reasoned. Rate limiting is an important protection against abuse, reducing the risk of attackers systematically probing the system or trying to overwhelm it to gain unauthorized access to sensitive data.

Now consider ongoing reviews and monitoring. Name a high-priority testing or validation measure you would institute, and explain its benefit for our scenario.

You: Prompt injection testing simulates malicious inputs designed to trick the chatbot into ignoring security rules or exposing sensitive HR information. Regular testing ensures the system remains resilient against prompt manipulation attacks.

Defense Policy Mentor: Strong answer. Regular prompt injection testing is vital for identifying and patching vulnerabilities specific to language models, especially for an HR chatbot handling sensitive data. It proactively helps maintain the integrity of enforced controls.

Please describe a second vital testing or validation approach and its impact on the HR chatbot’s security or compliance.

You: Bias testing evaluates the AI model’s responses to ensure it treats all employees fairly and does not produce discriminatory outputs. For an HR chatbot, this is critical for maintaining compliance with workplace policies and preventing biased or unfair treatment in HR-related interactions.

Defense Policy Mentor: Excellent addition. Bias testing is essential for any HR-facing system to uphold fairness, support compliance with workplace and legal standards, and protect against reputational or legal risks from unjust outputs.

You've completed all the key steps for developing a strong defense policy for our HR chatbot.
```
