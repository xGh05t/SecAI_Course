
# 6.2.6 Activity: Conduct a Risk Assessment

Your organization plans to implement a new AI-driven system. You have been asked to conduct a risk assessment, engaging with stakeholders to identify and explain the main risks associated with AI in this context.

Begin by consulting with the Information Security Officer and the AI Risk Stakeholder. Request a brief risk analysis report from each of them. Then, consolidate their findings into a single risk assessment report and share it with the AI Risk Auditor. Use your knowledge of AI risks and responsible AI principles to explain the risks you identify and suggest appropriate mitigations.

## Information Security Officer
```
Information Security Officer: Welcome. As the Information Security Officer, I can provide details about the security posture and operational risks of the planned AI system. You may request a brief security risk analysis report from me as part of your assessment.

You: Provide a structured AI security risk analysis report for the planned AI-driven system.

Include the following sections:

System Overview (assumptions about architecture, data flow, and integrations)
Key Security Risks (at least 6), including:
Data leakage / sensitive information exposure
Prompt injection and adversarial inputs
Model misuse or abuse
Unauthorized access / privilege escalation
Supply chain risks (third-party models, APIs)
Model inversion or data extraction attacks
Risk Severity (High/Medium/Low) with justification
Likelihood assessment
Recommended Mitigations for each risk, including:
Technical controls (e.g., input validation, output filtering, encryption)
Access controls (RBAC, least privilege)
Monitoring/logging strategies
Residual Risk after mitigation
Keep the response concise but detailed enough for an audit review.

Information Security Officer: Certainly. Here is a structured AI Security Risk Analysis Report for the planned AI-driven system:

1. System Overview The system is architected as a cloud-based AI platform that ingests user data through web and API interfaces, processes information using proprietary and third-party AI models, and delivers output to clients via secure endpoints. Data flows from user input, through preprocessing, model inference, and post-processing, before reaching storage and logs. Integrations include authentication services, monitoring tools, and third-party model APIs.

2. Key Security Risks

a. Data Leakage / Sensitive Information Exposure

Risk: Unauthorized access or bugs could reveal sensitive or regulated data in logs, model outputs, or storage.
Severity: High — Potential for significant organizational and legal impact.
Likelihood: Medium — With current controls, not common, but possible via misconfiguration.
Recommended Mitigations:
Encrypted storage; enforce least-privilege access
Strong output filtering and obfuscation routines
Periodic audits and monitoring of log access
Residual Risk: Low, if controls and monitoring are maintained.
b. Prompt Injection & Adversarial Inputs

Risk: Attackers manipulate AI model behavior by crafting malicious inputs (e.g., prompt injection).
Severity: High — Potential control over system output, reputational and security consequences.
Likelihood: Medium — Sophisticated attackers are expected.
Recommended Mitigations:
Rigorous input validation and sanitization
Limit model capabilities based on context
Continuous red-teaming/adversarial testing
Residual Risk: Medium, as new attack techniques may arise.
c. Model Misuse/Abuse

Risk: The system is intentionally leveraged for harmful or unauthorized purposes (e.g., generating phishing content).
Severity: Medium — May result in indirect reputational or compliance damages.
Likelihood: Medium — Open interfaces invite possible abuse.
Recommended Mitigations:
Monitor for anomalous request patterns or misuse
Implement content filtering and abuse detection
Restrict access to sensitive model functionalities
Residual Risk: Medium.
d. Unauthorized Access / Privilege Escalation

Risk: Attackers may gain unauthorized access, elevate privileges, or manipulate system configurations.
Severity: High — Unrestricted access can compromise the entire system.
Likelihood: Low — With robust access controls, unlikely but critical to secure.
Recommended Mitigations:
Multi-factor authentication (MFA), Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
Network segmentation and regular access audits
Residual Risk: Low, provided controls remain enforced.
e. Supply Chain Risks (Third-Party Models/APIs)

Risk: Compromised third-party models or APIs introduce vulnerabilities or malicious code.
Severity: High — Potential for systemic compromise or unauthorized data access.
Likelihood: Medium — Third-party reliance introduces known and unknown risks.
Recommended Mitigations:
Conduct formal risk assessment of third-party providers
Limit and monitor API/model access
Isolate untrusted components and track dependencies
Residual Risk: Medium, due to external dependencies.
f. Model Inversion or Data Extraction Attacks

Risk: Attackers use AI system outputs to infer or reconstruct sensitive training data.
Severity: High — Breaches confidentiality and may violate regulations.
Likelihood: Low — Requires sophistication and system-specific knowledge.
Recommended Mitigations:
Minimize exposure by restricting output detail
Differential privacy where feasible
Monitor for suspicious query patterns
Residual Risk: Medium, as full mitigation may impact system utility.
3. Summary Table

Risk	Severity	Likelihood	Main Mitigations	Residual Risk
Data Leakage	High	Medium	Encryption, access control, log monitoring	Low
Prompt Injection/Adversarial Input	High	Medium	Input validation, red teaming	Medium
Model Misuse/Abuse	Medium	Medium	Usage monitoring, abuse detection, content filtering	Medium
Unauthorized Access	High	Low	MFA, RBAC, network segmentation	Low
Supply Chain Risks	High	Medium	Third-party vetting, isolation, dependency management	Medium
Model Inversion/Data Extraction	High	Low	Output restriction, privacy controls, query monitoring	Medium
```

```
AI Risk Stakeholder: Certainly. Here is a structured AI risk analysis report from the compliance, privacy, and model risk perspective for the proposed AI-driven system:

1. Regulatory and Compliance Risks

Applicable Frameworks: The system must comply with GDPR and CCPA, particularly regarding consent, data minimization, user rights (access, erasure), and the handling of personal data. Internal data retention and acceptable-use policies are also in scope.
Data Residency & Cross-Border Transfers: The use of cloud-based infrastructure may involve cross-border transfers. Data residency requirements and mechanisms such as Standard Contractual Clauses should be confirmed and documented for all datasets containing personal information.
2. Data Governance Risks

Data Quality, Integrity, and Lineage: All datasets are cataloged and have a documented purpose. However, data quality checks must be consistently applied, and provenance should be validated to ensure accuracy and integrity.
Training Data - Consent, Licensing, Provenance: The training dataset includes personal data, and its use beyond the original purpose must undergo a formal review to ensure compliance with user consent and licensing agreements.
3. Bias and Fairness Risks

Potential for Biased or Discriminatory Outputs: The training data primarily reflects one region and customer segment. This could introduce bias, leading to degraded performance or disadvantages for underrepresented groups.
Impact on Protected Groups: There is a medium risk of disparate impacts; bias testing for demographic representation and outcomes is necessary.
4. Model Behavior Risks

Hallucinations and Inaccurate Outputs: The model performs well on historical test data but may not generalize to new or edge scenarios, risking inaccurate outputs.
Explainability/Transparency: The model’s complexity limits explanations to high-level feature importance, which constrains transparency into individual decisions.
Over-Reliance on Automation: If unchecked, over-reliance on AI recommendations may lead to accountability gaps or missed critical human judgment.
5. Ethical and Responsible AI Risks

Accountability Gaps: Current governance is not fully defined for documenting and tracking end-to-end AI decision processes.
Transparency: Model limitations in explainability hinder full transparency.
Human Oversight: Absence of human-in-the-loop for high-risk use cases may pose a risk.
6. Risk Severity and Impact

Regulatory/Compliance: Medium — strong foundational controls exist, but border cases and expanded data usage need review.
Data Governance: Medium — controls largely in place, but re-use of legacy data and data quality need ongoing attention.
Bias/Fairness: Medium to High — regional and demographic imbalances present moderate to significant risk.
Model Behavior: Medium — known performance issues for new conditions require mitigation.
Ethical/Responsible AI: Medium — accountability and oversight policies should be strengthened.
7. Recommended Mitigations

Governance Controls: Further enforce data cataloging, audit trails, and periodic compliance reviews.
Bias Testing and Monitoring: Regularly test model outputs for bias; ensure ongoing demographic analysis and corrective retraining if disparities are found.
Human-in-the-Loop: Mandate human review on critical decisions or where model outcomes are high-impact.
Documentation and Explainability: Maintain comprehensive documentation; use available model explainability tools; communicate limitations prominently to users and stakeholders.
Policy Alignment: Review and harmonize with responsible AI principles, focusing on fairness, accountability, transparency, privacy, and security.
```
