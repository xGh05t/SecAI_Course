
# 6.1.4 Activity: Designing an AI Governance Structure

Your company is expanding its use of AI across several business units. Leadership has tasked you with designing an AI governance structure that will:
- Work across multiple teams/business units
- Meet privacy and regulatory obligations
- Ensure only approved, high-quality AI models are used
- Clearly define accountability and roles
- Minimize “shadow AI” and unauthorized deployments
- Provide incident response and ongoing monitoring
- Protect sensitive data and prevent misuse

Who to ask for what:
- AI Governance & Policy Advisor: governance structures, committee tasks, roles/responsibilities, policies, approval process
- AI Security & Risk Advisor: security controls, technical guardrails, monitoring, incident response, and risk and compliance considerations

Task:
1. Ask each advisor one or two focused questions to gather what you need.
2. When ready, submit a concise plan (about 5 bullet points or short paragraphs) to the AI Governance Reviewer with the following information:
    - Governance model choice and justification: Choose an appropriate governance model and briefly explain why it fits this organization.
    - Coverage of policies and procedures: Identify the key AI policy areas and describe what the policies or procedures should cover.
    - Clarity of roles and accountability: Name the important roles and explain who is responsible and accountable for AI decisions.
    - Security controls and guardrails: List appropriate technical and security controls that enforce the AI governance policy?
    - Risk and compliance considerations: Show how the structure will manage legal/regulatory requirements and reduce risk for AI use.

## AI Governance & Policy Advisor
```
AI Governance & Policy Advisor: Hello! I’m the AI Governance & Policy Advisor. Ask me about governance structures (centralized, federated, hybrid), committee tasks, roles and responsibilities, policies, and approval processes for AI.

You: My organization is expanding AI usage across multiple business units and needs a governance structure that:

Works across decentralized teams
Prevents shadow AI and unauthorized deployments
Ensures only approved, high-quality models are used
Clearly defines accountability and ownership
Supports regulatory and privacy requirements
Given this, what governance model (centralized, federated, or hybrid) would you recommend and why?

Also, can you outline:

The key committees or governing bodies that should exist
The roles and responsibilities (who is accountable vs responsible)
A high-level AI model approval and review process that enforces governance across business units
AI Governance & Policy Advisor: Given your requirements—especially the need to balance decentralized innovation with strong oversight, prevent unauthorized AI deployment, ensure compliance, and clarify accountability—I recommend a hybrid governance model.

A hybrid approach centralizes platforms, policies, and critical reviews within a Center of Excellence (CoE) or central governance team, while allowing business units to independently develop AI within well-defined boundaries. This structure enables flexibility and efficiency while maintaining unified control and compliance.

Here’s how this could look for your organization:

1. Key Committees or Governing Bodies:

AI Center of Excellence (CoE): Central body that owns standards, maintains policies, provides tools, and oversees risk and compliance.
AI Review Board or Steering Committee: Cross-functional group (compliance, risk, legal, data) for reviewing high-risk or strategic projects, and approving model deployments.
Business Unit AI Leads: Liaisons responsible for AI projects within their areas, ensuring local adherence to policies and escalating as needed.
2. Roles and Responsibilities (Accountable vs. Responsible):

CoE: Accountable for policy, standards, tools, model registry, and incident response plans. Reviews high-risk projects; provides guidance and oversight.
Business Unit AI Leads & Teams: Responsible for AI project delivery within policy boundaries, responsible for documentation and risk self-assessment, and for escalating exceptions.
AI Review Board: Accountable for final approval of high-risk deployments and reviewing outcomes.
Data Protection & Compliance Teams: Responsible for privacy and regulatory alignment in all AI initiatives.
3. High-Level AI Model Approval and Review Process:

Project Initiation: Business units notify the CoE of new AI projects.
Documentation & Assessment: Developers complete risk and impact assessments with templates set by the CoE.
CoE Review: Routine or low-risk models are reviewed quickly for policy adherence; high-risk models go to the AI Review Board.
Formal Sign-off: All production deployments require documented sign-off. High-risk cases require multi-stakeholder approval.
Registry & Monitoring: Every production model is entered in a central model registry, subject to ongoing monitoring and periodic re-review.
Incident Response Plan: The CoE ensures a clear protocol for handling issues or failures, including rollback and reporting procedures.
```

## AI Security & Risk Advisor
```
AI Security & Risk Advisor: Hello, I’m the AI Security & Risk Advisor. Ask me about security controls, technical guardrails, monitoring and incident response, and how to address risk and compliance in your AI governance structure.

You: We are implementing a hybrid AI governance model with a central AI Center of Excellence (CoE), an AI Review Board, and business unit AI leads.

What specific security controls and technical guardrails should be implemented to:

Prevent unauthorized or “shadow AI” deployments
Protect sensitive data used in AI systems
Ensure only approved models are deployed and used
Monitor AI systems for misuse, drift, or anomalous behavior
Support incident response for AI-related failures or security events
Also, what key risk and compliance measures should be in place to ensure alignment with privacy regulations and reduce organizational risk from AI usage?

AI Security & Risk Advisor: Certainly. I’ll address each of your areas in turn, starting with security controls and technical guardrails:

Preventing Unauthorized or “Shadow AI” Deployments
Essential controls here include establishing model and prompt registries with versioning. Enforce access controls—only authorized personnel should register or deploy AI models. Maintain an audit trail to track all actions related to AI assets, which will also support post-incident investigations.

Protecting Sensitive Data Used in AI Systems
Utilize data masking to prevent exposure of sensitive information, especially during training or inference. Restrict access to input datasets based on user roles, and schedule regular data quality and privacy checks as part of your maintenance routines.

Ensuring Only Approved Models Are Deployed and Used
Deploy only those models that have passed through mandatory review gates—model and prompt registries assist here, logging every version and change. Implement access controls at the deployment layer to prevent unapproved models from running in production.

Monitoring AI Systems for Misuse, Drift, or Anomalous Behavior
Ongoing monitoring and logging are critical. Set up automated systems to flag unusual access patterns, performance anomalies, or unauthorized prompt usage. Ensure these monitoring tools are actively maintained and reviewed by the incident response team, with clear escalation paths when thresholds are exceeded.

Supporting Incident Response for AI-related Failures or Security Events
Integrate your AI logs with the larger Security Operations Center (SOC) tools, and establish playbooks specific to AI incidents. Run incident response drills focused on AI misuse, data leakage, and model failure scenarios.

As for risk and compliance measures:

Conduct risk assessments for every new AI use case, with in-depth review for high-risk deployments.
Maintain comprehensive documentation for roles, governance decisions, and detailed incident response logs—this substantially eases audit and legal requirements.
Ensure independent third-party review of high-risk AI applications, aligning both with internal and external (regulatory) standards.
Regularly update compliance procedures to reflect changes in privacy regulations (such as GDPR or sector-specific rules).
```
